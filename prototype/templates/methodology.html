Forensic Methodology & Epistemology

Foundational principles, competitive positioning, and technical limitations.

Scientific Rigor • Falsifiability

1. The Principle of Non-Accusatory Correlation

In real-world digital forensics, anomalous patterns frequently arise from benign operational events: unsynchronized Network Time Protocol (NTP) daemons, delayed syslog buffer flushes, automated cron jobs, or hardware sector reallocation.

TraceIQ Nexus enforces a strict cognitive barrier: a suspicious pattern is not proof of wrongdoing. The platform deliberately pairs every contradiction alert with plausible benign hypotheses and demands direct verification of physical and network corroboration before establishing causal links.

2. Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)

Adapted from Richards Heuer's seminal methodology for intelligence analysis, TraceIQ Nexus treats alternative scenarios as equally subject to falsification. The platform tracks supporting and contradicting evidence independently, refusing to convert tally counts into probabilistic truth values.

3. Cryptographic Integrity vs Legal Chain of Custody

While TraceIQ Nexus implements genuine SHA-256 cryptographic verification using Python hashlib, it explicitly documents that local hash matching does not constitute legally certified chain of custody. Real forensic workflows require certified write-blockers, sealed physical acquisition containers, and tamper-resistant cryptographic audit logs.

Key Technical Limitations
  • Synthetic Data: Case TQN-2026-001 uses purely fictional telemetry.
  • Deterministic Rules: Corroboration relies on explicit rule logic rather than statistical machine learning.
  • SQLite Storage: Local database can be edited by privileged local system accounts.
  • Single Investigator: Prototype is designed for single-analyst educational lab evaluation.
